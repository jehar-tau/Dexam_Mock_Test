If you are in school, you don't have eight free hours a day for UCEED. You have tired evenings, weekends that fill up fast, and school tests that never seem to end. This plan is built for that life. It asks for about 90 minutes on school days and three to four hours on weekend days, and it still gets you through twelve weeks of steady, structured work.

Before you start, check this year's brochure for the exam date and count back twelve weeks. If you have more time, stretch the first phase. If you have less, shorten it, but keep the last four weeks as they are.

## What you are preparing for

Know the paper before you plan for it.

- **Part A** is computer-based and lasts 120 minutes. It has 14 numerical-answer questions (4 marks, no penalty), 15 multiple-select questions (4 marks, partial credit for a correct subset, −1 for any wrong option) and 28 single-answer MCQs (3 marks, −0.71 for a wrong answer).
- **Part B** is 60 minutes on paper, with two tasks: drawing and design aptitude.

Three things follow from this:

1. **Numerical questions carry no penalty.** Always enter an answer, even a careful estimate.
2. **Multiple-select questions punish any wrong option.** Mark only the options you are sure of. A correct subset still earns partial credit.
3. **MCQs cost you for a wrong guess.** Answer when you can rule out enough options to feel confident, and skip when you can't.

Practise every Part A paper under these rules from week 1, so that by exam day the rules feel natural.

## Your weekly time budget

A normal school week looks like this:

- **Monday to Friday:** 90 minutes a day. Three blocks of about 30 minutes.
- **Saturday:** 3 to 4 hours.
- **Sunday:** 3 hours, with one block kept for review and planning.
- **One evening off.** Choose it now and protect it.

That is roughly 13 to 14 hours a week. It is not huge, but it is consistent, and consistency is what moves your scores.

### A school-day block

1. **30 minutes: drawing.** Sketch from life. Objects on your desk, a corner of your room, family members reading or cooking. Draw what you see, not what you think it looks like.
2. **30 minutes: Part A practice.** Ten to fifteen questions on one topic, timed.
3. **30 minutes: review.** Check every answer. For each mistake, write one line on why you got it wrong.

If you have only 45 minutes on a hard day, do 15 minutes of each. Never skip the review. It is where the learning happens.

### A weekend block

- **Saturday:** one full Part A section or paper under the real timing, then a one-hour review.
- **Sunday:** one Part B session (both tasks, 60 minutes), then 30 minutes comparing your work with your previous attempts, then 30 minutes planning the coming week.

## Weeks 1–4: build the base

The goal of these four weeks is to understand the paper and build daily habits.

- **Week 1:** Attempt one full UCEED past paper, Part A and Part B, without worrying about the score. Note which kinds of questions felt hardest.
- **Week 2:** Spend your weekday Part A blocks on the hardest kind from week 1. Draw every day, focusing on simple objects and proportion.
- **Week 3:** Move to the second-hardest kind of question. Start drawing people and objects in a setting, not just alone.
- **Week 4:** Mix all the kinds of questions you have practised. On Saturday, attempt a second full Part A and compare it with week 1.

By the end of week 4, your mistakes notebook should show patterns. Use them to decide what weeks 5 to 8 focus on.

## Weeks 5–8: build speed and judgement

Now you practise under pressure and start making decisions about what to answer.

- **Week 5:** Cut your time on weekday sets by about a third. If you did 15 questions in 30 minutes, try 15 in 20.
- **Week 6:** Practise multiple-select questions separately. For each one, decide which options you would mark, then check how the −1 rule would have scored you.
- **Week 7:** Practise MCQs with a skip rule. Before answering, ask yourself whether you have ruled out enough options to feel confident. Track how many skipped questions you would actually have got right.
- **Week 8:** One full past paper on Saturday under real conditions. Review it on Sunday, both parts.

During these weeks, push Part B harder. Give yourself 30 minutes per task in your Sunday sessions, which matches the 60 minutes for two tasks. Finish both, even if the second is rough.

## Weeks 9–12: full papers and polish

The last four weeks are about full papers, review and rest.

- **Week 9:** Two full papers, one on Saturday and one on Sunday. Weekday blocks are all review and targeted drills.
- **Week 10:** Two full papers again. Look at your marks by question type. Find the type where you lose the most to penalties and practise it on weekdays.
- **Week 11:** One full paper on Saturday. Sunday is review and light drawing only.
- **Week 12:** Short weekday sessions. One last Part B practice early in the week. The two days before the exam are for light sketching, sleep and getting your things ready.

## When school tests arrive

You will have unit tests, practicals and pre-boards during these twelve weeks. Plan for them instead of panicking.

Switch to **maintenance mode** during a heavy school week:

- 15 minutes of drawing a day.
- 15 minutes of Part A practice on most days.
- No full papers.
- Move that week's weekend work to the next weekend if you can, or simply skip it.

Maintenance mode keeps your skills warm without stealing time from school. After the test week ends, go back to the normal plan exactly where you left off. Don't try to catch up by doubling your hours.

## A simple weekly review

Every Sunday evening, answer these five questions in your notebook:

1. Did I do at least five of my six study days?
2. Which kind of question cost me the most marks this week?
3. Did I lose marks to penalties? On which question type?
4. Is my drawing better than it was two weeks ago? Compare the pages.
5. What is the one thing I will focus on next week?

Keep the answers short. The point is to notice patterns, not to write an essay.
