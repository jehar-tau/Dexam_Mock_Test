Thirty days is not enough time to become a different artist. It is enough time to become a much calmer, sharper and better-paced version of the one you already are. That is what this month is for.

Here is what we know about the test. NID DAT B.Des. Prelims is a pen-and-paper test in English, with text and visual prompts. Your admit card gives the duration. Shortlisted candidates move on to Mains, and its format is in each year's handbook. For anything else, including the date, reporting time and what you may carry, check this year's handbook and your admit card.

Everything below is built around those facts: paper, pen, English prompts, and a fixed time you must learn to use well.

## Before day 1: set up your month

Spend one evening on this. It makes the next four weeks much easier.

- **Find your time limit.** Use the duration on your admit card. If it is not out yet, use the time printed on the past papers you practise with, and switch once the admit card arrives.
- **Gather your materials.** Keep the pencils, pens, erasers, sharpeners and colours you plan to use in one pouch. Check the admit card or handbook for what is allowed, and practise only with those.
- **Collect past papers.** Aim for at least six papers you have not attempted yet. Keep them unopened until their day.
- **Make a mistakes notebook.** Every practice session ends with a few lines in it.
- **Fix your daily slot.** Pick a time of day close to your expected test time, so your brain gets used to working then.

## Week 1 (days 1–7): find out where you stand

The goal this week is an honest audit, not a good score.

1. **Day 1:** Attempt one full past paper under real conditions. Paper, pen, timer, phone in another room.
2. **Day 2:** Review it slowly. For every question, write one line: what it asked, what you did, and what a stronger answer would have done.
3. **Day 3:** Sort your notes into groups. Common ones are reading the prompt wrongly, running out of time, weak drawing, a thin idea, and messy presentation.
4. **Days 4–6:** Spend each day on your biggest group. If time was the problem, practise short timed tasks. If ideas were the problem, practise generating many rough options quickly.
5. **Day 7:** Light day. Sketch from life for 30 minutes and rest.

## Week 2 (days 8–14): drill your weak spots

Now practise the parts, not the whole. A good daily block for this week takes about three hours:

- **20 minutes: warm-up.** Quick sketches of objects around you. Hands, bottles, chairs, people at a distance. No erasing.
- **60 minutes: prompt practice.** Pick three or four questions from past papers that match your weak groups. Do each under a strict time limit.
- **20 minutes: break.** Walk, stretch, eat. Not your phone.
- **45 minutes: second round.** Redo one of today's questions from scratch, using what you learnt in the first attempt.
- **30 minutes: review.** Compare both attempts. Write your three biggest lessons in the mistakes notebook.

On day 14, attempt a second full paper. Compare it with day 1, not with anyone else.

## Week 3 (days 15–21): full papers and pacing

This is your heaviest week. The aim is to make the full test feel familiar.

- **Days 15, 17 and 19:** Full papers under real conditions.
- **Days 16, 18 and 20:** Review the previous day's paper for an hour, then do one hour of targeted drills.
- **Day 21:** Rest. Read through your mistakes notebook once, and that is all.

While you do full papers this week, practise a pacing routine:

1. **Read the whole paper first.** Give it a few minutes. Mark the questions you feel strongest on.
2. **Plan a rough time budget** for each question, based on how much space and detail it asks for.
3. **Write the budget at the top of each question** in small, light pencil if that is allowed, or keep it in your head.
4. **Move on when the time is up.** A finished, decent answer usually beats a perfect half-answer.
5. **Keep a few minutes at the end** to check that every question has something on the page.

## Week 4 (days 22–30): taper and get ready

You don't cram for a drawing test. You arrive rested, with steady hands.

- **Days 22 and 24:** One last full paper each, then a short review.
- **Days 23, 25 and 26:** Short sessions of 90 minutes. Warm-up, two timed questions, review.
- **Days 27 and 28:** Only light sketching and reading your mistakes notebook.
- **Day 29:** Sort out logistics, then rest.
- **Day 30:** Test day.

If you feel you have not done enough, resist the urge to add a full paper on day 28. A tired hand on test day costs more than one extra practice paper gives you.

## Reading English prompts carefully

Prelims gives you text and visual prompts, and a surprising number of marks are lost to misreading. Use this routine on every question, in practice and on the day:

1. Read the prompt once, fully, without picking up your pencil.
2. Underline the action words. Draw, show, design, list, explain, label.
3. Circle the limits. A number of ideas, a viewpoint, a size, colours or no colours.
4. Ask yourself: "What exactly would a full answer contain?" Then start.

If the prompt has a picture, look at it for a full ten seconds before you read the text again. Details in the image often matter.

## Working well on paper

On a pen-and-paper test you cannot undo, copy or resize. Practise the habits that protect you.

- **Start light.** Rough out your layout with light lines before you commit to dark ones.
- **Plan your space.** Decide where the main drawing goes before you begin, so it does not run off the edge.
- **Keep your hand clean.** Rest your drawing hand on a scrap sheet to avoid smudging.
- **Label clearly.** If you write notes on a drawing, keep them short and neat.
- **Colour last.** If you use colour, add it only once the drawing is finished.

## The day before and the morning of

The day before:

- Put your admit card, and anything else it tells you to bring, in one place.
- Pack your materials pouch. Sharpen every pencil. Test every pen.
- Check the route and travel time to the centre.
- Do 20 minutes of easy sketching. Then stop.
- Sleep at your normal time.

The morning of:

- Eat a proper breakfast.
- Leave early enough to arrive calmly.
- Loosen your hand with a few finger stretches while you wait to go in.
- When the paper arrives, read it all before you start.

## Try this week

- Find your time limit and write it on the first page of your mistakes notebook.
- Pack your materials pouch and practise with only what is in it.
- Attempt one full past paper under real conditions; the year-wise papers on DMocks work well for this.
- Use the four-step reading routine on every prompt this week.
- Book one full rest day and keep it.
