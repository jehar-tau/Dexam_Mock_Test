Here is a pattern almost everyone falls into at some point. Question 7 looks solvable. You start. Two minutes pass, then four. You are sure you are nearly there, so you keep going. Eventually you get it, or you do not, and either way you have spent the time of three easier questions. At the end of the paper you are rushing through questions you could have done calmly.

The two-pass method is a simple fix. You go through the paper twice. The first pass collects every mark you can take quickly. The second pass goes back for the harder ones, with whatever time is left. It sounds obvious. It only works if you practise it until it is automatic.

## The idea in one paragraph

On the first pass, answer anything you can solve within a fixed time limit. Anything that takes longer gets marked and left. You sort the marked questions into two bins as you go: **soon** (you can see a path, it just needs time) and **maybe** (you have no clear path yet). On the second pass you work through the soon bin. In the final minutes you deal with the maybe bin, using the negative-marking rules for each question type.

## Your pass-one time limit

You need a ceiling, a point at which you stop and move on. It should be a little below your average time per question, so that the first pass leaves real time for the second.

- **UCEED Part A:** 120 minutes for 57 questions is about 126 seconds per question. Use a **90-second** ceiling on the first pass.
- **CEED Part A:** 60 minutes for 44 questions is about 82 seconds per question. Use a **60-second** ceiling on the first pass.

The ceiling is not "time to finish". It is "time to see a path". If you have a clear path at 60 seconds and need another 30 to finish, finish. If you are still staring at the question at the ceiling, mark it and go.

## A starting timetable

Treat these as starting points. Adjust them after two or three mocks.

**UCEED Part A (120 minutes)**

1. Minutes 0 to 70: first pass through every question.
2. Minutes 70 to 105: second pass through the soon bin.
3. Minutes 105 to 120: the maybe bin, then a final check that every numerical-answer question has something entered.

**CEED Part A (60 minutes)**

1. Minutes 0 to 35: first pass through every question.
2. Minutes 35 to 52: second pass through the soon bin.
3. Minutes 52 to 60: the maybe bin and the final check.

Write these clock times at the top of your rough sheet as soon as the paper starts. Converting minutes into actual clock times takes ten seconds and saves a lot of mental arithmetic later.

## If sections are timed separately

Check this year's brochure and the official mock interface to see whether you can move freely between sections or whether each section has its own clock. If sections are locked, run two passes *inside* each section.

One fair way to split time is by marks. Using the marks listed for each question type:

- **UCEED:** numerical answers carry 14 × 4 = 56 marks, multiple-select 15 × 4 = 60 marks and single-answer MCQs 28 × 3 = 84 marks, 200 in all. Splitting 120 minutes by marks gives roughly 34, 36 and 50 minutes.
- **CEED:** numerical answers carry 8 × 4 = 32 marks, multiple-select 10 × 4 = 40 marks and MCQs 26 × 3 = 78 marks, 150 in all. Splitting 60 minutes by marks gives roughly 13, 16 and 31 minutes.

Within each section, keep the same shape: about 60 per cent of the time on pass one, 30 per cent on pass two and 10 per cent on the final sweep.

## Keep the bins on paper

Do not rely on memory or only on the interface's review flag. On your rough sheet, draw two columns, **soon** and **maybe**, and write question numbers in them as you go. At the start of pass two you can see your whole workload at a glance.

Most computer-based interfaces have a "mark for review" button. Read the instructions on how a question that is both marked and answered is treated, and use it the same way in every mock, so there are no surprises on the day.

## What goes in which bin, by question type

The rules differ because the marking differs.

- **Numerical answer (no penalty):** if you can work it, it goes in the soon bin. Before the paper ends, every numerical-answer question should have something entered, even a rough estimate, because a blank earns nothing and a wrong answer costs nothing.
- **Multiple-select (partial credit, −1 for any wrong option):** on the first pass, mark only the options you are sure of and move on. Adding an option you are unsure about risks turning partial credit into −1. Come back in pass two only if you can confirm another option.
- **Single-answer MCQ:** if you can rule out at least one option, it is worth attempting in pass two or the final sweep. With four options, rule out one and the expected value is positive in both exams: 3 × 1/3 − 0.71 × 2/3 is about +0.53 in UCEED, and 3 × 1/3 − 0.5 × 2/3 is about +0.67 in CEED.

## Three mistakes that break the method

1. **A slow first pass.** If you spend the full ceiling on most questions, pass one eats the whole paper. Pass one should feel brisk, slightly uncomfortable even.
2. **A huge soon bin.** If more than about a third of the paper lands in the soon bin, your ceiling may be too tight or you are being too cautious. Look at which questions you marked and solved easily in pass two.
3. **Changing answers without a reason.** On the second pass, only change a first-pass answer if you find a specific error. A vague feeling is not enough.

## How to practise it

The method feels awkward at first, because walking away from a question you have half-solved is uncomfortable. That discomfort is the habit forming.

- **Week one:** practise on half-papers. Take a set of questions, give yourself half the real time and run two passes. Use a stopwatch on the desk.
- **Week two:** do one full paper with the timetable written on your rough sheet. Afterwards, check how many soon-bin questions you solved in pass two and how many maybe-bin questions you got right.
- **Week three:** adjust the ceiling. If pass two always runs out of time, tighten the ceiling by 10 or 15 seconds. If pass two finishes early, loosen it.

Review each practice paper with the same question every time: did the method get me more marks than one straight pass would have?

## Try this week

- Work out your clock-time checkpoints for your exam and write them on a card.
- Do one half-paper with a 90-second ceiling for UCEED or a 60-second ceiling for CEED.
- Keep soon and maybe columns on your rough sheet and count what landed in each.
- After the session, note how many soon-bin questions you solved on the second pass.
- Run one full timed paper with the method, then adjust your ceiling by no more than 15 seconds.
- Year-wise UCEED and CEED papers on DMocks are one place to practise the timetable under real conditions.
