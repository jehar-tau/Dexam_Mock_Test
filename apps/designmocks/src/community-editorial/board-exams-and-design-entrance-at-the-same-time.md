Class 12 is already a heavy year. Adding a design entrance exam on top can feel like trying to run two races at once. It is possible to do both well, but not by working twice as hard every single day. You do it by deciding, week by week, which race gets most of your energy, and by keeping the other one ticking over.

First, get the facts. Depending on the year, your board exams and your entrance tests may fall within weeks of each other, or even overlap with practicals and pre-boards. Check this year's brochure for every entrance exam you plan to sit, and your school or board timetable for exam dates. Write them all on one calendar before you plan anything else.

## Know what you are balancing

Each design exam tests something a little different.

- **UCEED** has a computer-based Part A of 120 minutes with numerical, multiple-select and single-answer questions, and a 60-minute Part B on paper with drawing and design aptitude tasks.
- **NIFT B.Des.** has a computer-based GAT of 120 minutes with 100 MCQs, and a CAT on paper that tests observation, concept, colour and illustration.
- **NID DAT B.Des.** Prelims is pen-and-paper, in English, with text and visual prompts. Shortlisted candidates go on to Mains, set out in each year's handbook.

What they share is drawing on paper, careful reading and working to a clock. Those three habits are your bridge between boards and design prep. Careful reading helps your board answers too, and the timed practice you do for one exam makes the other feel less frightening.

## The three-phase calendar

Divide your year into three phases. The exact weeks depend on your dates, but the shape stays the same.

### Phase 1: build (until your pre-boards start)

This is when design prep can take a real share of your time.

- **School days:** about 60 to 90 minutes of design prep after homework.
- **Weekends:** 3 hours on one day, with one full past paper every second weekend.
- **Rough split:** around 60% boards, 40% design across the week.

Use this phase to learn the formats, fix your weakest areas and build a daily drawing habit. Everything you build now makes the later phases easier.

### Phase 2: split (pre-boards and the weeks around them)

Now boards need more of you, but design prep should not stop.

- **School days:** 30 to 45 minutes of design prep. Fifteen minutes of drawing plus one short timed set.
- **Weekends:** 90 minutes on one day.
- **Rough split:** around 80% boards, 20% design.

The aim is to stay sharp, not to improve fast. You are protecting the skills you built in phase 1.

### Phase 3: focus (the final weeks before each exam)

In the last stretch before any exam, that exam gets nearly all of your time.

- **Before a board paper:** design prep shrinks to 15 minutes of sketching a day. It doubles as a break.
- **Before an entrance exam:** boards drop to light revision, and you spend your study time on full papers, review and rest for the entrance test.

If a board paper and an entrance exam fall in the same week, plan that week day by day. Write the plan down the moment you know the dates.

## Daily time blocks that work

Here are three sample days. Adjust the times to your school hours.

### A phase 1 school day

1. School, then rest and food.
2. **Two hours:** board homework and revision.
3. **20-minute break.**
4. **30 minutes:** a timed set for your entrance exam, under its real marking.
5. **20 minutes:** review that set and note your mistakes.
6. **20 minutes:** sketch from life before bed.

### A phase 2 school day

1. School, then rest and food.
2. **Two and a half to three hours:** board revision.
3. **15 minutes:** quick sketching.
4. **20 minutes:** a short timed set, ten questions or one small drawing prompt.

### A phase 3 day before a board paper

1. Board revision as your teachers suggest.
2. **15 minutes:** light sketching as a break. Nothing timed, nothing stressful.

## Keeping drawing alive in 15 minutes

Drawing fades fastest when you stop, and it is the one skill all three exams share. Even in your busiest weeks, keep 15 minutes a day for it.

- **Draw what is in front of you.** Your textbook, a water bottle, your sibling on the sofa. No setup needed.
- **Draw in your margins.** A rough sketch at the end of a revision block counts.
- **Draw people.** Short gesture sketches of family members moving around the house build speed.
- **Draw from memory.** Close your eyes, picture your school gate, then draw it. Compare later.
- **Colour once a week** if you are sitting NIFT, since the CAT tests colour.

## Handling the pressure at home

Many students feel caught between parents and teachers who want full focus on boards, and their own wish to prepare for design. A clear plan helps everyone.

- **Show your calendar.** When your family can see the three phases and the board weeks clearly marked, it is easier for them to trust your design time.
- **Keep your board marks steady.** Nothing builds trust like keeping up in school.
- **Ask for one small thing.** A quiet hour, a corner of the table, or help with errands in exam weeks.

## A Sunday check-in

Spend ten minutes every Sunday on these:

1. Which phase am I in this week?
2. Did my time split match the phase?
3. Did I draw on at least five days?
4. Is anything coming up in the next three weeks? A pre-board, a practical, an entrance date?
5. What one change will I make next week?

Adjust your plan every week. A plan that never changes was probably never followed.

## Try this week

- Check this year's brochure for each entrance exam and put every date, board and entrance, on one calendar.
- Mark your three phases on that calendar.
- Draw for 15 minutes every day, even on your busiest day.
- Attempt one timed set under the real marking of your entrance exam; the Learn section and year-wise papers on DMocks have sets you can use.
- Show your calendar to someone at home and agree on your design hours.
