Most students don't struggle with one exam. They struggle with four names that all seem to say "design", and a quiet fear of picking the wrong one. The good news is that the four papers are different enough that, once you look at how each one actually works, the choice gets much clearer.

This guide covers what each exam asks of you on the day, what kind of preparation each one rewards, and a short trial you can run to decide for yourself. Dates, eligibility, fees and the list of institutes that accept each score can change from year to year, so check this year's brochure for all of those.

## First question: undergraduate or postgraduate?

This splits the four straight away.

- **CEED** is the M.Des. entrance from IIT Bombay. It is the postgraduate route.
- **UCEED** (the B.Des. entrance from IIT Bombay), **NID DAT B.Des.** and **NIFT B.Des.** are undergraduate routes.

If you are in school, your real decision is between the three B.Des. exams, and many students sit more than one. If you are a graduate or close to graduating, read the CEED part below, check this year's brochure for who is eligible, and then go straight to the trial.

## What each paper looks like on the day

### UCEED

Part A is computer-based and lasts 120 minutes. It has three kinds of question:

- 14 numerical-answer questions, 4 marks each, no penalty.
- 15 multiple-select questions, 4 marks each, with partial credit for a correct subset and −1 if you pick any wrong option.
- 28 single-answer MCQs, 3 marks each, with −0.71 for a wrong answer.

Part B is 60 minutes on paper, with two tasks: drawing and design aptitude.

**What it rewards:** careful reading under time pressure, and knowing when not to answer. The marking scheme is part of the test. A student who understands it will often beat a student who simply answers more.

### NIFT B.Des.

The GAT is computer-based, 120 minutes, with 100 single-answer MCQs. A right answer gets +1, a wrong one −0.25, and a blank gets 0. That works out to a little over a minute per question. The CAT is on paper and tests observation, concept, colour and illustration.

**What it rewards:** a steady pace across a long run of questions, and comfort with colour and illustration on paper.

### NID DAT B.Des.

Prelims is a pen-and-paper test in English, with text and visual prompts. Your admit card gives the duration. Shortlisted candidates go on to the Mains stage, and its format is set out in each year's handbook.

**What it rewards:** thinking on paper, answering open prompts in your own visual way, and reading English instructions closely. There is no option list to fall back on.

### CEED

Part A is computer-based and lasts 60 minutes. It has 8 numerical-answer questions (4 marks, no penalty), 10 multiple-select questions (4 marks, partial credit, −1 for any wrong option) and 26 MCQs (3 marks, −0.5 for a wrong answer). That is 44 questions in one hour.

Part B is 120 minutes: five sketching and design tasks, 20 marks each. If you split your time evenly, that is about 24 minutes per task.

**What it rewards:** one fast, accurate hour, followed by two hours of sustained sketching and design thinking.

## Five questions to ask yourself

Take a page and answer these honestly. Don't write what you think a designer should say.

1. **Do I enjoy timed objective questions, or do I come alive with a blank page?** UCEED Part A, NIFT GAT and CEED Part A are objective. NID prelims and the drawing parts are open.
2. **How do I react to negative marking?** UCEED, CEED and the NIFT GAT all take marks away for wrong answers, each by a different amount. If that makes you freeze, you will need deliberate practice under each paper's own rules.
3. **How much do I draw right now?** Every one of these exams has a paper-based drawing or design part. If you draw rarely, that is the skill to start this week, whichever exam you choose.
4. **Which programmes do I actually want?** Read the programme pages and brochures of the institutes you are considering. Pick the course first and the exam follows.
5. **How many hours a week do I really have?** Count them, then subtract school or work, travel, sleep and one rest evening. That number decides how many exams you can prepare for properly.

## Sitting more than one exam

There is real overlap between the B.Des. exams, which is why many students sit two or three.

- **Drawing practice carries over.** Daily sketching helps UCEED Part B, the NIFT CAT and NID prelims alike.
- **Objective practice carries over, with a warning.** UCEED Part A and NIFT GAT both test you on screen, but their marking differs. Always practise each paper under its own rules, or you will build the wrong guessing habits.
- **Reading carries over.** NID prompts are in English, and every objective paper rewards reading the question twice.

A simple way to split your time:

- Pick one **home exam**. It gets about 60% of your preparation time.
- Pick a **second exam** that shares the most with it. It gets about 30%.
- Keep about 10% for a third, only if you genuinely want it.

If two exam dates fall close together, your home exam wins the final fortnight. Check this year's brochures for the dates before you commit.

Don't sit four exams because a friend is. Each one adds forms, fees, travel and a different paper to learn. Two exams prepared well usually serve you better than four prepared thinly.

## A two-week trial to decide

If you still can't choose, run this trial. It costs two weeks and saves months of doubt.

1. **Days 1–2:** Read this year's brochure for each exam you are considering. Note eligibility, dates and the institutes that accept the score on one page.
2. **Day 3:** Attempt one UCEED Part A past paper in 120 minutes, under its real marking.
3. **Day 5:** Attempt one NIFT GAT past paper in 120 minutes, under its real marking.
4. **Day 7:** Attempt one NID prelims past paper on paper, with the time limit printed on that paper or in that year's handbook.
5. **Day 9:** Do two drawing tasks from any of the papers, timed.
6. **Day 11 (graduates):** Attempt one CEED Part A in 60 minutes and two Part B tasks in about 24 minutes each.
7. **Days 12–14:** Review. For each paper, rate three things out of five: how much you enjoyed it, how tired you felt at the end, and how clearly you can see what to improve.

Don't judge by your first score. A low score with a clear path to improve is a better sign than a decent score where you have no idea what went right. The exam that scores highest on enjoyment and on room to improve is a strong candidate for your home exam.

- Write "B.Des." or "M.Des." at the top of a fresh page and list the exams that match.

- On Sunday, choose a home exam and a second exam, and write down why.
