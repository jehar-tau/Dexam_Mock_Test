# 8. The owner's reference standard

On 13 November 2026 the owner supplied the question bank that defines what
"hardest" means for spatial topics. **From now on the top tiers of every
spatial programme are calibrated against it.** It overrides any easier
reading of the difficulty ladder.

The owner's words: "It is not just a cube. It has multiple faces, and it is
a complex three-dimensional object which is folded and unfolded. When I ask
for a higher difficulty level, this is what I am expecting. The toughest
should match this level, and going forward you should take this as the
reference."

## 8.3 The anchor questions for folding and unfolding

The owner named four questions as the level to reach. Described in our own
words:

| Anchor | What is shown | What makes it hard |
|---|---|---|
| UCEED 2015, Q76 | A solid made of two many-sided prisms joined by a band of rectangles, and four long nets with pentagon and hexagon faces attached to a strip of about a dozen rectangles. | About twenty faces. The options differ only in where three or four polygon faces attach along the strip. The student must count faces by type, find the strip, and check which strip segment each cap attaches to and on which side. |
| UCEED 2016, Q27 | An L-and-T shaped solid built from cubes, and four cross-shaped nets with dotted fold lines. | Faces are merged rectangles of different sizes, not unit squares. The four nets are nearly identical: one arm is a square longer or sits one step across. Edge lengths must match around every fold. |
| UCEED 2016, Q33 | A stepped block with a sloping face that runs into a long rounded end, and four irregular nets with rounded tabs. | A profile with steps, a slope and a curve, extruded. The curved surface unrolls into a rectangle as long as the arc. Options differ in the order of strip segments and which side the end caps sit on. |
| UCEED 2023, Q49 | Two box-like pieces joined at an angle, with triangular faces between them, and four nets drawn on a square grid with diagonal fold lines. | Triangles and squares mixed. A diagonal fold means a sloping face. Options are mirror images and one-step shifts of each other. |

Other forms in the same group that the top tier must also reach:

- A cuboid (not a cube) with stripes that must line up across fold lines.
- A cube whose faces carry a continuous curve or a split-colour pattern;
  select every net that folds to it (multiple select).
- Two views of a cube with a blotchy pattern: which net is it.
- A solid shown from front and back with eight-sided and four-sided faces.
- A triangular-prism package with tabs and slots: which blank cannot make it.
- A hollow box cut along two planes: which unfolded pieces are right.
- A cross-shaped solid of five cubes with coloured faces, folded from two
  strips and two crosses.
- A net that is partly folded: which partly folded picture is wrong.

What a student must be able to do at this level:

1. Read an unfamiliar solid: count its faces and sort them by shape.
2. Find the belt: the run of faces that wraps round the solid and unrolls
   into a strip, and the order of its segment lengths.
3. Attach the caps: which belt segment each end face joins, and on which
   side of the strip.
4. Match edge lengths across every fold.
5. Reject mirror images.
6. Do all of that on four options that differ in one face, in about two
   minutes.

## 8.4 The level in the other subtopics that have been read

**Cube rotation (dice).** A die's net plus a stack of four dice: sum of the
faces parallel to the ground. Several regular dice stacked on a table,
seen from two sides: the greatest possible sum of all visible faces. Six
views of one cube in a rolling series: the next view. Two views with
labelled faces and three "opposite" statements: which net. A solved
3×3×3 puzzle cube after four named face turns: which picture. Our dice
specification covers the first two forms; the series, the labelled
opposites and the face-turn puzzle are not yet specified.

**Surface counting.** Solids are not cube stacks. Two views of a
three-armed bracket with recesses: count surfaces. A block drilled by a
cylinder through slots: count surfaces, including curved ones. Four views
of a machined part with grooves and half-round channels: count surfaces. A
cube with a pyramid removed, then another: how many surfaces are possible.
A pyramid with triangular holes right through. A chamfered cube with
notches: vertices plus edges. A hot cube dragged along a bent path through
a wax block: surfaces left. Our surfaces specification stops at cube-based
solids with tunnels; it must grow to sloped, curved and drilled solids.

**Orthographic views.** The solids are interlocking bars, brackets with
slots, branching pipes and multi-coloured assemblies, each shown from one
or two directions; pick the same object from another direction among four
that differ in one arm or one mirror flip. Also: three views of a house
block with seven printed shapes; a ball's seam from four sides, find the
top; top and front given, select every object that fits; three identical
circle-in-square views, the fewest surfaces. Our views specification
(cube-built solids) reaches the bar and bracket forms only if solids grow
to 10–16 cubes with protruding arms; printed-face solids and curved
solids are not yet covered.

## 8.5 What this means for work already built

Honest position on 13 November 2026:

- **Cube nets**, as built and as first specified, tops out at single cubes
  with symbols. That is the exam's easy end. The anchor level needs a new
  engine for general solids. It is specified in
  [topics/nets-of-solids.md](topics/nets-of-solids.md) and is now the top
  of the folding ladder.
- **Counting cubes** (the reference programme) is correct for its topic
  and reaches the exam's hardest form for cube stacks (fewest cubes from
  three views). It stays the reference for **structure**: tiers, lesson
  anatomy, typed answers, hints, worked solutions. It is not the reference
  for how hard a spatial question can be.
- **Surfaces of solids**, **views and rotation** and **dice**
  specifications each need a further Exam hard lesson set, listed in 8.4,
  before they are built.

## 8.6 Rule for every builder from now on

Before calling an Exam hard or Mastery tier built:

1. Open the matching subtopic in the owner's bank, or its description
   here. Pick the three hardest questions.
2. For each, name the generated kind that demands the same reasoning with
   the same number of faces, parts or steps. If there is none, the tier is
   not done.
3. Put a generated question beside the anchor. If the generated one is
   visibly simpler (fewer faces, options that differ in obvious ways, a
   plain cube where the exam uses a compound solid), raise it.
4. Record the comparison in the topic specification's coverage table.
