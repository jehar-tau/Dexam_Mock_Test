# Community editorial articles: style and format

Editorial articles live in `src/community-editorial/<slug>.md`, one per file. They are
published under the owner's name (decided 28 Nov 2026) and appear first in Community →
Articles, above articles written by students.

## File format

```
---
slug: how-to-review-a-mock-test
title: How to review a mock test (the review matters more than the score)
dek: One sentence, at most 180 characters, that tells the reader what they will get.
exams: all            # or a comma list from: uceed, ceed, nid, nift
theme: papers         # one of: planning, papers, mindset, skills
cover: target         # one of: clock, calendar, sketch, eye, cube, path, calm, conversation, papers, compass, balance, home, bulb, target
---

Body in the Community Markdown subset.
```

The body may use only `## Heading`, `### Subheading`, `- bullet`, `1. numbered`,
`> quote`, `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `---` rules and plain paragraphs. No links,
images, tables, HTML, code or `#` level-one headings. Keep it between 900 and 1,500 words.

## Voice

- Write for a 16–22-year-old preparing alone, often with no design teacher nearby. Be warm, direct and specific. Use short sentences and second person.
- British/Indian spelling ("practise" as a verb, "programme", "colour").
- Give methods the reader can try today: steps, timings, small exercises, checklists.
- First person is fine ("I'd start with…"), but invent no biography, anecdotes, ranks, cut-offs, statistics, results, quotes or student names. If a fact is not in the sources below, say "check this year's brochure" instead of guessing.
- Phrase topics students ask about on Quora, Reddit and Medium in our own words. Copy no one's text.
- End with a short "Try this week" list. At most one sentence may mention practising on DMocks (year-wise papers, Learn or All India mocks). Never mention prices.
- No phone numbers, emails, @handles or social links. These are blocked in Community.

## Exam facts we stand behind

From `docs/EXAM_RULES.md` and `src/exam-guides.js`, checked against the 2027 brochures:

- **UCEED (B.Des., IIT Bombay):** Part A is computer-based, 120 minutes. It has 14 numerical-answer questions (4 marks, no penalty), 15 multiple-select questions (4 marks, partial credit for a correct subset, −1 for any wrong option) and 28 single-answer MCQs (3 marks, −0.71 for a wrong answer). Part B is 60 minutes on paper: two tasks, drawing and design aptitude.
- **CEED (M.Des., IIT Bombay):** Part A is computer-based, 60 minutes. It has 8 numerical-answer questions (4 marks, no penalty), 10 multiple-select questions (4 marks, partial credit, −1 for any wrong option) and 26 MCQs (3 marks, −0.5 for a wrong answer). Part B is 120 minutes: five sketching and design tasks, 20 marks each.
- **NIFT B.Des.:** The GAT is computer-based, 120 minutes, with 100 single-answer MCQs (+1 for a right answer, −0.25 for a wrong one, 0 if left blank). The CAT is on paper and tests observation, concept, colour and illustration.
- **NID DAT B.Des.:** Prelims is a pen-and-paper test in English with text and visual prompts. The admit card gives the duration. Shortlisted candidates go on to the Mains stage; its format is in each year's handbook.
