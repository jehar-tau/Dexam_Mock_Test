# Part B — Drawing Studio

Four original 30-minute prompts for design-entrance practice. They are written to exercise the kinds of thinking a Part B paper rewards: observation, imagination, spatial planning, communication, and a clear process.

These prompts are **original**. They are not copied from Roughworks, UCEED, CEED, NID, or NIFT.

## How to use this pack

1. Pick one prompt.
2. Set a 30-minute timer.
3. Work on paper with a pen/pencil. Do not use reference images unless the prompt allows it.
4. Spend the first 3–5 minutes thinking and composing; do not start with details.
5. At the end, write three lines: what the idea is, what the viewer should notice first, and what you would improve with another 10 minutes.
6. Score the sheet with the rubric below, or ask a peer to score it blind.

---

## Prompt 1 — Courtyard reading nook

**Time:** 30 minutes  
**Brief:** A community library has a narrow 3 m × 6 m courtyard. Design a reading nook for three people that works in both sun and light rain. Show a plan, one perspective, and one construction detail.

**Think about:** orientation, shade, drainage, seating for one/two/three, a place to put a book and a bag, privacy without isolation, and how the structure is built.

**Suggested sheet layout:** plan at 1:50, perspective, one enlarged detail, short labels.

**Review criteria**

- Clear response to the site and weather.
- Usable circulation and seating.
- Structural logic and material choice.
- Drawing clarity and composition.

---

## Prompt 2 — Tactile stationery sorter

**Time:** 30 minutes  
**Brief:** Design a handheld tool that helps a visually impaired student identify and sort three types of stationery by touch. Show the form, the grip, and how the tool is used.

**Think about:** tactile differentiation without colour, one-hand use, orientation, safety, cleaning, cost, and how a user knows which end is which.

**Suggested sheet layout:** three use steps, two views of the tool, a grip detail, and a tactile coding diagram.

**Review criteria**

- Inclusive thinking and empathy.
- Tactile differentiation.
- Ergonomics and usability.
- Clarity of use.

---

## Prompt 3 — Foldable festival stall

**Time:** 30 minutes  
**Brief:** A festival stall sells two traditional snacks and one drink. Design a low-cost stall that one person can assemble in 15 minutes. Show the open and closed states.

**Think about:** transport, one-person assembly, stability, weather, serving height, storage, waste, and branding.

**Suggested sheet layout:** closed state, open state, assembly sequence, one detail, and a short cost/material note.

**Review criteria**

- Structural feasibility.
- Ease of assembly.
- Visual appeal and identity.
- Cost and material awareness.

---

## Prompt 4 — Repair café identity

**Time:** 30 minutes  
**Brief:** Create a visual identity for a neighbourhood repair café. Include a logo, one poster, and one wayfinding sign. Explain your choices.

**Think about:** the audience, the tone (practical, welcoming, not corporate), a simple mark that works small, a type choice, a colour system, and how the identity behaves across three surfaces.

**Suggested sheet layout:** logo construction, poster, sign, and a short rationale.

**Review criteria**

- Concept and relevance.
- Visual consistency.
- Typography and layout.
- Communication.

## Rubric

Score each criterion from 0 to 5. A total of 20 is a strong practice sheet; 25 is excellent.

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2–3 | 4–5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Idea and relevance** | Does not answer the brief | Answers the brief but generically | Specific, original, and clearly connected to the brief |
| **Composition and communication** | Confusing layout; viewer cannot find the idea | Readable but crowded or under-annotated | Clear hierarchy; the idea is legible in 5 seconds |
| **Drawing and form** | Weak proportion, perspective, or construction | Competent but inconsistent | Confident, controlled, and purposeful drawing |
| **Process and reflection** | Final image only | Some evidence of exploration | Shows decisions, alternatives, and a reason for the final choice |

### Quick self-review

- Can a stranger tell what the idea is in five seconds?
- Is the most important thing the biggest thing?
- Does the drawing show how the thing is used or built?
- Are the labels doing real work, or just decorating?
- What would I change with 10 more minutes?

---

## 30-minute time plan

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Read the brief twice; underline the constraints. |
| 3–7 min | Explore 3 thumbnail ideas; choose one. |
| 7–12 min | Lay out the sheet; establish scale and main view. |
| 12–24 min | Draw the main view, supporting views, and one detail. |
| 24–28 min | Add labels, arrows, and a short rationale. |
| 28–30 min | Check the brief; fix the darkest value or the weakest label. |

## A note on scoring

Part B is judged by humans, and reasonable evaluators can disagree. The goal of this rubric is not to pretend that drawing is objective. It is to make your own review more specific: “the composition is unclear” is less useful than “the eye goes to the empty corner before the main figure.”
